# The Ontoject, One Life

The trumps of the Ontoject deck can be read as one life: the development of a single being, from the egg that waits before conception to the body going back into the soil. Birth falls in the middle of the deck, at XII and XIII. Everything from the zero to XI happens before anyone meets you.

This is one reading, not the meaning of the cards. The trump order was fixed by card makers centuries before anyone saw an embryo under a microscope, and the tradition did not intend it. It is offered the way the deck itself is offered: made, and known to be made.

**[Open the animated page →](studies/ontoject-one-life.html)** Three cards drawn as moving pictures (the Void, Death, Harmony) and all twenty-two trumps as glyphs morphing one into the next, with the Trickster stepping in between every card.

The cards themselves, with every section, are in [the Ontoject deck](../viewers/cards.html?src=../tarot/ontoject-illustrated/grammar.json). This study follows that deck's names and order, which are those of *The Ontoject: A Tarot of Playful Process* (January 2026).

## Why development fits this deck

The deck already speaks this language. The Void is "pregnant potential". The Void Birthing Light spreads its golden circles "from a dark womb". Vitality's two overlapping circles are "the womb of creation". Death is a boundary dissolving, and Harmony is a spiral of beings, some ascending, some descending, with a point of light travelling through them. This reading takes those images at their word.

Development is also a place where subject and object will not stay apart, which is what the word *ontoject* is for. The placenta is grown by the embryo and lives inside the mother. And it runs both ways: cells from a pregnancy can stay in the mother's body for decades. One study found a son's cells in his mother's blood as long as 27 years after he was born ([Bianchi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.93.2.705)). Some of the mother's cells stay in the child too.

It is recursive, too. The egg that became you was formed inside your mother while she was herself a fetus inside hers. The zero card is already one generation deep.

## The shape

Birth falls at XII and XIII, where the tradition puts the Hanged Man and Death. That is a real proportion: the body is laid down fastest before birth.

- **Water, 0 to XI:** before conception, conception, the embryo, the fetus, labour.
- **The crossing, XII and XIII:** born upside down; the placental life ends and the breathing life begins.
- **Air, XIV to XXI:** from the first feed to the return.
- **The Trickster, unnumbered:** play, present at every stage.

## The trumps as one life

The third column is what makes this a cosmology and not a timeline: at each stage the being lives in a different world, and the world grows with it.

| | The card | The stage | The world | What happens, loosely |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The Void (First Zero) | Before conception | Another body | The egg waits, paused partway through its division, as it has since before its carrier was born. Nothing is yet this one. |
| 0 | The Void Birthing Light (Second Zero) | Fertilisation | The egg itself | A sperm meets the egg and a calcium wave crosses it from the point of entry. The egg finishes its division, the shell around it hardens so no second sperm enters, and two half-sets of chromosomes begin to approach. |
| I | Self-Realization | The zygote | One cell | One cell carrying a genome that has never existed before. The card says "I am"; the biology says "one". |
| II | Subject–Object Duality | Cleavage, the blastocyst | A hollow ball | One becomes two, four, eight, without growing. By about day five, the first split of fates: an inner cluster that will become the body, and an outer layer that will become most of the placenta, the part that faces the other. |
| III | Time | Implantation | The mother's body | The blastocyst attaches to the wall of the womb and announces itself in a hormone (hCG, what pregnancy tests detect). It enters someone else's calendar, which clinics count from about two weeks before conception: the clock was running before the being existed. |
| IV | Structures | Gastrulation | A disc becoming a body | Cells move through a groove and sort into three layers. The body plan is laid down, and each layer's possible futures narrow: structure as enabling constraint. Lewis Wolpert said that gastrulation, not birth, marriage or death, is the most important time in your life. |
| V | Inspiration from Eternity | The first heartbeat | A pulse | Around three weeks after conception, heart cells begin to contract on their own. No nerve tells them to; the rhythm starts inside the tissue. |
| VI | The Cross | Neural tube and segments | An axis | The neural plate folds into a tube along the back (the vertical), while paired blocks of tissue are laid down on either side in rhythm (the horizontals). Spine and ribs to come. |
| VII | The Spiral | Looping | A curl | The straight heart tube bends into a loop, the first plain left–right difference in the body. The embryo curls; the cord will twist into a helix. The cross set in motion. |
| VIII | Balance | The fetus moving | Water | Organs are in place and now grow. The fetus moves weeks before anyone can feel it, and the inner ear's balance organs take shape. Exchange with the mother runs both ways. |
| IX | Silence | Hearing | Muffled sound | From around the sixth month the fetus responds to sound: the mother's heartbeat, gut and voice. Newborns prefer their mother's voice ([DeCasper and Fifer 1980](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7375928)), so the listening has gone on for months before any speaking. |
| X | Destiny | Turning into the path | A narrowing passage | Most babies turn head-down near term, then rotate through the pelvis: the body turns to fit the way out. What is given meeting what is done with it. |
| XI | Strength | Labour | Contractions | Force that is not the baby's will: the mother's body pressing, the baby yielding. Strength as the lion held gently. |
| XII | Sacrifice | Birth | Air and light | Born, usually head first, the Hanged Man's posture. Then the placenta, grown from the embryo's own cells, is let go: the first part of oneself given up. |
| XIII | Death | The first breath | Lungs | The lungs fill. The shortcuts that let blood bypass them before birth begin to close within minutes to days. Two circuits, lungs and body, begin to run through one heart. Something dies so the breathing one can live. |
| XIV | Temperance | Nursing, co-regulation | A caregiver's body | Milk poured from one body into another. The infant's heart rate, warmth and stress are steadied by being held. Two vessels, one flow. |
| XV | Facing Existential Threats | Childhood meets death | A family, a street | Somewhere around five to seven, most children grasp that death is permanent and comes to everyone. A five-year-old asks whether you will die too. |
| XVI | Impermanence | Adolescence | Peers | Puberty remakes the body; the brain prunes connections it does not use into the twenties. The child's tower comes down. What survives becomes identity. |
| XVII | The Star | Young adulthood | A life of one's own | The courage to be oneself, as the card says: vocation, choosing, being seen as a particular person. |
| XVIII | The Moon | The middle | The same world, stranger | Anxiety without an object; paths that looked clear go dim. Whether a midlife low is common is argued over in research; the card does not need it to be universal. |
| XIX | The Sun | Generativity | The next generation | The courage to be as a part, as the card says: giving that renews itself, raising, teaching, making things for people who come after. |
| XX | Vitality | Old age and dying | The edge | Being and nonbeing overlapping, the vesica: an old body holding more of both. The slot where the tradition puts Judgement, a call. |
| XXI | Harmony (The Ontoject as Immanence) | Return | Everything | The body's matter goes back into soil, microbes, other lives; what was passed on goes on spiralling through others. |
| ☆ | The Trickster | Play | Any | A fetus kicking for no reason, a toddler's game, an adult's joke. No number, as the Fool once had none. |

## Where the fit is forced

At **I**, "I am" at one cell can be read as a claim about when a person begins. This reading makes no such claim, and the card should not carry it. An alternative is the moment, a few days in, when the embryo starts reading its own genes instead of the egg's stored instructions.

At III, implantation fits "subject meets object" better than "time". A more literal match is the segmentation clock, genes switching on and off in cycles that lay down the body's segments like ticks, but it comes in weeks three to five and would sit out of order.

At V, the self-starting heartbeat is an image of grace arriving from inside. It is a weaker image of Palmer's receptive listening, which the card also carries.

VIII is the loosest fit. The placenta's two-way exchange could be the balance itself: in the card's words, "dynamic equilibrium", not "static perfection".

At X, if birth should come sooner, put it here (the Wheel turns, and you land in a family, a place, a time you did not choose) and move labour and the first breath forward. That gives adulthood more cards.

XVIII to XX carry midlife, generativity and old age loosely, borrowing Erik Erikson's adult stages more than biology. Biology has less to say about these decades than about the first nine months.

## The Trickster, the Fool and the Joker

Wasn't the Fool the Joker before? Not in the history, though the two are often confused.

In the old Italian game the Fool, *il Matto*, was not a trump at all. It carried no number and stood outside the sequence. Its job in play was to excuse you: you could play it at any time instead of following suit or playing a trump, which is why it was called *l'Excuse* in French (Farley 2009, p. 82). In the Tarot de Marseille it is still unnumbered (p. 113).

The Joker comes from somewhere else. It was added to ordinary playing-card packs in the United States in the mid-nineteenth century as an extra top trump for the game of euchre. Farley dates it to around 1857 and argues that it cannot be a remnant of the tarot trumps: the Fool was never a trump, ordinary packs existed decades before tarot and had no Joker, and the two cards do different things in their games (pp. 8, 82). The World of Playing Cards gives the same origin, with American euchre players in the 1860s and the first Jokers in American packs around 1863 ([Wintle, "The Joker Card"](https://www.wopc.co.uk/jokers/index)). It notes that some historians have seen the Joker as a descendant of the Fool, and that others reject it.

The Fool's number came later, from the occultists. Court de Gébelin, in 1781, called it zero "though it is placed in play after XXI", because it counts for nothing alone and gives value to the other cards ([his essay, as translated on this site](../viewers/cards.html?src=../tarot/court-de-gebelin-tarot/grammar.json)). Eliphas Lévi put it between Judgement (XX) and the World (XXI), and often numbered it XXI (Farley, p. 113). By 1911 Waite's *Pictorial Key* lists it as "Zero. The Fool", the numbering most decks follow today.

The Ontoject deck splits the Fool's two roles. **0 is the Void**, the before; the Trickster keeps the old unnumbered freedom: it "exists outside the sequence, able to appear anywhere". In January the deck labelled the Trickster "Joker". The history above does not make the Joker the Fool's heir, but a deck can choose that name knowingly, the way it chooses everything else.

![The Trickster, from The Path of the Ontoject](https://pub-71ebbc217e6247ecacb85126a6616699.r2.dev/grammar-image-gen/1772564947670-r6tculit6we.png)

*The Trickster, from The Path of the Ontoject, the deck's other edition: "the playful, ever-present spark dancing through the cosmos—divine play (lila) and creative chaos."*

The Path of the Ontoject also tells the whole path as a story, and in it the Trickster comes right after Subject–Object Duality: "with awareness, movement begins." Development has a match there. Around day five or six the blastocyst hatches, squeezing out of its shell before it implants. The escape artist, between II and III. Or the Trickster stays where the Fool once stood, outside the count: play just before the return, or anywhere at all.

## Reversed: hubris

The deck has one rule for reversals, and the Trickster carries it. In PlayfulProcess's words:

> "The Trickster has two sides: equanimity or balance, and the reversed is hubris. All cards follow this pattern for the reversed: it is hubris in whatever polarity, an exaggeration, outside of what the Buddha would call the middle path."

So a reversed card is the same card overdone, pushed out of the middle way in one direction or the other. The Trickster upright is equanimity, play that keeps things light; reversed, it is hubris.

The cards already say this in places. Balance asks for "not too much karma (doing), not too much dharma (being)", and warns that "too much individuality becomes isolation; too much participation becomes loss of self": two ways to fall off the same card. The Cosmic Topology edition of *The Path of the Ontoject* writes its reversals the same way. Strength reversed is "brute force", Temperance reversed is "pouring too much or too little", and its Hubris card, reversed, becomes "paralysis from fear of hubris": hubris in the other polarity.

On the [animated page](studies/ontoject-one-life.html), the Trickster steps in between every card in play. It copies the card it follows, overdoes it, wobbles, and slips into the next: a clown's imitation, which is what a reversal looks like. The *Reversed* button draws every card that way.

## What this reading does not say

It does not say the embryo replays the cosmos, or evolution. Ernst Haeckel's "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" is rejected in its strong form, and some of his embryo drawings were altered to fit it. If his plates are ever used for these cards, the claim should not ride along with them; that would be Court de Gébelin's Egypt, made with a microscope.

It does not say the card makers meant this, or that these stages are the cards' true meaning. And it does not settle when a person begins.

## Where the order matters

The deck keeps the Marseille order, with Balance in Justice's slot at VIII and Strength at XI. Under the Golden Dawn's swap, Strength moves to VIII and Justice to XI, and this reading would put labour in the middle of pregnancy and the fetus's balance at birth. The map depends on the Marseille order.

The early Italian orders also varied by city before the numbered sequence settled ([IPCS pattern sheet 001](https://i-p-c-s.org/pattern/PS001.pdf)). Reading one life into one fixed order is a choice made on top of a convention. Which is what the deck says it is.

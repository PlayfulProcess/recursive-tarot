# Book T: a Community Deck

Book T is the Golden Dawn's own book of the tarot, printed in 1912 and now in the public domain. Anyone may use its words. The pictures in this deck are a first set, made for now and meant to be improved, and I would like some of the next ones to come from other hands.

So this deck is open in one direction: you can add to it. A new picture for a card, a whole style, a correction, a translation. I review every contribution before it appears.

## What you can add

**A picture for one card.** Pick a card, read what Book T says about it, and draw or make that. Your picture appears beside the deck's own picture for that card, with your name.

**A whole style.** A set of cards in one hand or one look: your linocuts of the four Aces, a Major Arcana in watercolour. Give the style a name, so the cards travel together.

**A correction.** The pictures here were made with AI and then checked against Book T, card by card. Some still miss a detail: a count of swords, a crest, a hand. If a picture says something Book T doesn't, tell me which words it misses. The same goes for the text: if the transcription differs from the [1912 scan](https://archive.org/details/IAPSOP-equinox_v1_n8_1912_Sep), give the page.

**A translation.** Every card carries Book T's passage in English. A translation into a language you read well lets someone else read the card in theirs.

## The rules

**Book T's words decide every symbol.** Draw what the passage says: the number of swords, the colour of the robe, which hand holds what. Where you add something Book T doesn't mention, keep it small. When you send your picture, quote the passage you drew from.

The twenty-two Keys are the exception. Book T gives each Key a title and a meaning, and no picture. For a Key, quote the title and say where your picture comes from.

**Leave lettering out of the picture.** No painted titles, numbers, Hebrew letters or planet signs. The deck sets each card's name as text under the picture, and painted letters come out garbled, especially from image models.

**Hand-made and AI-made are both welcome. Say which.** If you used an image model, name it, and send the prompt. If you drew it, say with what: ink, gouache, linocut, a tablet.

**Credit yourself.** Give the name you want printed under the picture.

**CC BY-SA 4.0.** The deck is under this licence, so your picture will be too: anyone may reuse it, with your name, under the same licence. Send only what you have the right to give: your own drawing, or an image made from your own prompt. Not a trace of a modern deck.

**Shape.** Portrait, about 2 by 3 (the deck's paintings are 832 by 1248 pixels). JPG, PNG or SVG, under 1 MB.

## How to send it

The way that works today is a pull request on GitHub, the website where this deck lives. You need a free GitHub account and nothing else. A pull request is public: your GitHub name shows on it.

**Sending a picture (no code):**

1. Find your card's id. Open the card in the [card browser](../viewers/cards.html?src=../tarot/book-t/grammar.json) or in [the deck's file](https://github.com/PlayfulProcess/recursive-tarot/blob/main/tarot/book-t/grammar.json): the id is the short name like `swords-05` or `key-13-death`.
2. Name your file `<your-style>--<card-id>.jpg`, for example `pond-linocut--cups-ace.jpg`.
3. Open the [plates folder](https://github.com/PlayfulProcess/recursive-tarot/tree/main/tarot/book-t/plates), choose **Add file → Upload files**, and drop your picture in. GitHub makes your own copy of the project for you.
4. Choose **Propose changes**, then **Create pull request**, and fill in the note below.

```
Card: cups-ace
Book T's words I drew from: "A WHITE Radiating Angelic Hand, issuing from clouds..."
Style: Pond linocut
Made by: hand (linocut, printed and scanned)   or   AI (model name; prompt below)
Credit as: Your Name
I agree to share this under CC BY-SA 4.0: yes
```

5. I look at it. I may ask for a change. When I accept it, I add it to the card and it appears in the deck.

**Doing the whole step yourself.** If you're comfortable editing a text file, add your picture to the card in the same pull request: open `tarot/book-t/grammar.json`, find your card's id, and add one entry to its `illustrations` list:

```json
{
  "url": "https://tarot.recursive.eco/tarot/book-t/plates/pond-linocut--cups-ace.jpg",
  "artist": "Your Name (linocut, by hand)",
  "edition": "Pond linocut",
  "license": "CC-BY-SA-4.0"
}
```

Leave `is_primary` off: the deck's own picture stays the main one, and yours shows under it.

**Corrections and translations** go the same way, without a picture: edit the card's text in `tarot/book-t/grammar.json`, or open an issue saying which card, which words and which page. If GitHub is new to you, the [Ways to Contribute](course-viewer.html?course=how-to-contribute) course walks through it from the start.

## I review every contribution

Nothing appears in the deck until I accept it. I read each picture against Book T's words, the same way I check my own. I may ask you to change a detail, and sometimes I'll say no: a no is about this deck, not about your picture, and the picture stays yours to use anywhere under its licence.

## Ideas, if you want a place to start

- **A hand-drawn Major Arcana.** Book T gives the Keys titles and no pictures, so the twenty-two Keys have the most room for a reading of your own.
- **A style per element.** One person takes Fire, someone else Water. Four hands, one deck.
- **Linocut or woodcut.** Book T's pictures are emblems: a hand from a cloud, a rose, a count of swords. They cut well.
- **One card, many hands.** Pick a card the deck gets wrong, or one you love, and draw your version of it.
- **A children's style.** Simple shapes, soft colour, the same symbols.

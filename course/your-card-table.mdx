# Your Card Table

This is a practice I use to set priorities. It is the most ordinary thing on this site: a table, a deck, and an hour. I share it because it has changed how I relate to the cards. Here they don't tell me what will happen. They sit in a grid I drew, and I decide what to do with each square.

## The practice

Draw a table. Down the side, the **areas** of your life you want to look at: projects, practices, relationships, whatever is asking for attention. Across the top, three columns: **do less · keep · do more**. Shuffle, and lay one card in each cell. Sometimes two.

Then sit with it. The first thing I look at is where the **major arcana** fall, because those are the cards the tradition treats as the big movements. Then I go row by row. A card under *keep* in an area where I have been restless can read as a nudge to stay the course a little longer. A card under *do more* can surprise me. I don't look for the "best" card. I look for the one that feels **needed**, and I circle it. Those circled cards are the message of the table.

Under the table I sometimes write a line or two ("cards = possibilities") and, now and then, a short row from another deck or oracle beside the tarot.

The table is mine. The cards land in it; I decide. That is the whole creed of this site in one practice: [relate to the card, never obey it](course-viewer.html?course=how-tarot-works).

## The notation, in five minutes

To write a table down, or to type one, I use a short code for each card:

- **Major arcana** are Roman numerals: `0` (the Fool) to `XXI` (the World). `XVIII` is the Moon.
- **Minor arcana** are rank + suit. Ranks: `A` (or `1`), `2` to `10`, `P` page, `Kn` knight, `Q` queen, `K` king. Suits: `W` wands, `C` cups, `S` swords, `P` pentacles. So `4C` is the Four of Cups, `KnP` the Knight of Pentacles, `PP` the Page of Pentacles.
- An arrow **down**, `↓`, after a card means it fell reversed: `7C↓`. (`r` works too.) Upright needs no mark.
- **Parentheses** mean circled, the card carrying the message: `(XVIII)`.
- Two cards in one cell: a space between them, `8C 3P`.

By hand I draw the suits as small pictures: a leaf for wands, a heart for cups, a sword with its cross-guard for swords, a pentagram for pentacles. A hand-drawn sword can look like a `+`, so the page reads `+` as swords too.

A whole table is one line per area, cells separated by `|`:

```
columns: less | keep | more

Work      | 4C      | 3W      | (XVIII)
Health    | 7C↓     | 8C 3P   | QS
Writing   | (XVII)  | AS      | 6W
Family    | KnP     | (VIII)  | IV

> cards = possibilities
```

These rows are made up. A real table names real people and projects, so mine stay private.

## Try it

The **[Card Table](../viewers/table.html)** page does all of this in the browser:

- Add, rename or remove areas and columns, then **Lay the table**: one card falls into each empty cell from the deck you choose. Or type the cards yourself, from a reading you laid on the floor.
- Majors get a gold frame, circled cards a ring, reversed cards turn upside down.
- Tap a card to read what its deck says about it (the reversed reading first, when it fell reversed), circle it, turn it, and write **My note**.
- **Show codes** puts each card's code under it while you learn the notation. Untick it when you know them.
- Your table stays in your browser. Export it as a text file to keep it.

The codes are also a way to hand a table to an assistant: written this way, a table can be read, compared with last month's, or kept, without anyone having to guess which card is which. That part lives in recursive.eco, and it is still being built.

## What it is not

It is not a forecast. The cards don't know which project will succeed. What the table does is what the [other courses here](course-viewer.html?course=what-a-reading-can-do) describe: it puts a random picture next to a part of your life and asks you to say what you see. You may find a new angle, or you may find you already knew. Either way, the decision was yours before the cards fell, and it is yours after.

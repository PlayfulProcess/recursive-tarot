# 36 Tattvas — Image Briefs for Original Plates

2026-08-08. Companion to the PD-image pass on `tarot/thirty-six-tattvas/grammar.json` (7 cards received verified public-domain/CC0 Commons images: `concept-spanda`, `concept-trinity`, `tattva-35` Jala/Water, `tattva-01` Śiva, `shakti-cit`, `tattva-05` Śuddha Vidyā, `realm-psyche`). This document covers the other 49 cards — every one still needs an original plate. These briefs are precise enough to hand to an illustrator or an image model; they are NOT reproductions or paraphrases of any copyrighted source. Each is grounded in (a) that card's own existing `Yantra` section text already in the grammar, (b) standard tantric/Śaiva iconography — element colors, bindu, lotus, triangle, square, crescent — and (c) the deck's own accumulation doctrine (`concept-tanmatra-accumulation`) where relevant.

## Two constraints this system must satisfy

**Light-ground, not navy.** `tarot.recursive.eco` is light-only site-wide (see repo `CLAUDE.md` — one `theme.css`, no dark stages, backgrounds always light). The deck's current navy/dark plates are effectively invisible in the site's own chrome. Every brief below assumes a light parchment/cream or white ground with the yantra rendered in fine line-work and a single accent color per card — the inverse of the current dark-field cards, not a re-color of them.

**Photos next to diagrams — the coherence risk, and how these briefs handle it.** The 7 PD images just applied are museum photography and a 19th-c. lithograph; the 49 briefs below describe geometric line-diagrams (yantras). Sitting side by side in one deck, "photo of a bronze" and "line diagram of a triangle" can read as two different decks glued together. This system handles it with a shared *plate* convention rather than trying to make the diagrams look photographic or the photos look diagrammatic:

- **One frame, one caption style, one margin** for every card regardless of image type — a thin rule border and consistent caption typography (already how the viewer presents card images) does most of the coherence work by itself.
- **One palette governs both.** The five element colors used throughout this deck (deep blue = ākāśa, green-gray = vāyu, red = tejas, silver = jala, gold = pṛthvī) are already latent in the PD photos themselves — the Nataraja and Ardhanārīśvara bronzes read warm/gold-red, the Ganges lithograph reads silver-blue, the liṅgam reads warm stone, the Kashmir manuscript reads gold-and-ink. Keep the new original plates' accent colors in that same family so the two image types feel drawn from one restrained palette, not two unrelated mood boards.
- **Line-diagrams stay geometric, not decorative.** No painterly rendering, no attempt to "illustrate" a scene — every original plate is an abstract yantra (circle/square/triangle/lotus/bindu), which is categorically distinct from the photographs (real objects) rather than a weaker imitation of them. Distinctness-by-category reads as intentional; a near-miss at photorealism would not.
- **Consistent line weight and paper texture** across all 49 new plates (fine, single-weight linework, warm off-white ground) so the *new* cards cohere tightly as a set even before touching the older photos.

## Shared visual system for the 49 original plates

- **Ground:** warm parchment/cream (not pure white — echoes old palm-leaf/manuscript tradition and sits closer in warmth to the PD photos than stark white would).
- **Line:** fine, single-weight, dark warm-grey or deep brown ink line — never black. Bindu points and key accents get the one saturated element color for that card; everything else stays line-only.
- **Palette (inherited from the grammar's own existing color notes, do not invent new ones):** ākāśa/space = deep blue · vāyu/air = green-gray · tejas/fire = red · jala/water = silver · pṛthvī/earth = gold. Pure-tattva and covering cards inherit their element's color via their associated śakti (see each card's own `metadata.shakti`/`element`).
- **Motion vocabulary:** unmeṣa (expansion) reads as outward-radiating, opening lines; nimeṣa (contraction) reads as inward-nesting, tightening lines; "both" cards (air, water) show one gesture flowing into the other within the same plate.
- **Density vocabulary (accumulation doctrine):** the lower a tattva sits in the 36, the more visual "weight" — more rings, more layered color, denser line — consistent with `concept-tanmatra-accumulation`'s own claim that each element keeps every quality of the ones above it and adds one more.

## The Five Śaktis (level 1 — universal powers)

**`shakti-ananda` — Ānanda Śakti (Bliss).** Eight-petal lotus, bindu at center, petals just beginning to open rather than fully bloomed (this is bliss at its *source*, not yet expressed in a tattva). Fine silver-grey line on parchment; the faintest warm blush at each petal tip is the only saturated color. Motion: a slow, continuous outward bloom, radially symmetric, unhurried.

**`shakti-iccha` — Icchā Śakti (Will).** A single upward-pointing triangle with a bindu at its apex. Red linework, with the color deepening toward the apex as if concentrating there — will as gathering intensity rather than spread. No other ornament; this is the sparest of the five śakti plates, appropriately, since will here is pure impulse before it has taken any shape.

**`shakti-jnana` — Jñāna Śakti (Knowing).** A thin circle with twelve fine lines radiating just past its own edge, evenly spaced — knowing that touches everything equally, without preference for any direction. Green-gray line, cool and even; no line is thicker or longer than another (this is the diagnostic detail that separates it from the directional, unequal rays used later on the perception-sense cards).

**`shakti-kriya` — Kriyā Śakti (Action).** A square rotated 45° into a diamond orientation, bindu at center, fine diagonal hatching inside the square suggesting motion held within a stable frame ("dynamic movement within stability," per the card's own Yantra text). Gold line, slightly heavier weight than the other four śakti plates — action is the one that has visible weight.

*(`shakti-cit` already carries the copper Śrī Yantra PD photo — see Part 1.)*

## The Pure Tattvas (1–5, realm: pure)

**`tattva-02` — Śakti Tattva.** Twelve-petal lotus, bindu at center. Distinguish from `shakti-ananda`'s eight-petal lotus by petal count and by tone: pale rose-gold rather than silver, since this card is the universe's *first* stirring of self-recognition (vimarśa), warmer and more awake than ānanda-śakti's still-latent bliss.

**`tattva-03` — Sadāśiva Tattva.** Upward triangle, bindu at apex — same base shape as `shakti-iccha` but rendered sparer and more tentative: thinner line, smaller bindu, the barest wash of red rather than saturated color. This is will as vague stirring ("I am this," not yet specific), so the plate should look less resolved than the śakti card it echoes.

**`tattva-04` — Īśvara Tattva.** A circle containing an inverted (downward-pointing) triangle. Crisp, resolved linework — sharper corners, more confident line than Sadāśiva's — because this is the tattva of clarity: "This am I," the universe now seen (not just stirred toward). Gold-green line, the circle slightly thicker than the triangle to read as "wholeness of perception" containing Śakti's power.

*(`tattva-01` and `tattva-05` already carry PD images — see Part 1.)*

## The Kañcukas / Coverings (6–12, realm: covering)

All seven share one family of forms — a square with nested concentric circles inside, sized and colored per the card text, growing larger tattva by tattva (the text itself specifies "slightly larger," "still larger," "the largest kañcuka square" in sequence). Render all seven at one consistent line weight so the *only* variable that changes across the set is size and color, making the progression legible at a glance across the seven cards.

**`tattva-06` — Māyā.** A square inscribed inside a circle, five horizontal bands crossing the square like layered scrims or veils. Deep violet-purple wash (per the card's own text: "purple because all five śaktis are being limited simultaneously"). The five lines should read as translucent layers, not solid bars — veils, not walls.

**`tattva-07` — Kalā.** Square containing three concentric circles, gold line — the smallest and tightest of the seven kañcuka plates (limited action = the first, most contained contraction).

**`tattva-08` — Vidyā.** Same motif, one size larger, green-gray line.

**`tattva-09` — Rāga.** Same motif, larger again, silver line.

**`tattva-10` — Kāla.** Same motif, larger still, deep blue line — per the card's own text this is "the deepest contraction," so the concentric rings should feel most compressed/tightest-packed of the middle five even as the outer square grows.

**`tattva-11` — Niyati.** Same motif, the largest of the five kañcuka squares, red line.

**`tattva-12` — Puruṣa.** Visually returns to `tattva-06`'s composition (square in circle, veiling lines) per the card's own text ("the same pattern as Māyā"), but here a small bindu is clearly visible at the very center, faintly lit — consciousness never actually lost, only wrapped. The five veiling lines each carry a whisper of one kañcuka color (gold/green-gray/silver/blue/red, very desaturated) rather than Māyā's single purple — all five coverings now bound together into one self.

## Prakṛti — the Pivot (13, realm: material)

**`tattva-13` — Prakṛti.** A circle with five thin concentric rings and a bindu at center. Unlike every other ringed card, the color here is not one of the five elements but a quiet three-way balance — three faint background tints (a cool pale tone, a warm pale tone, a neutral grey-brown) interleaved almost imperceptibly, evoking the three guṇas (sattva/rajas/tamas) in perfect unmanifest equilibrium, not yet churning into anything below. This is the one plate in the deck that should look *poised*, not moving in either direction.

## The Inner Instrument (14–16, realm: material)

Three plates sharing one base form — a circle containing an upward triangle with a bindu — differentiated only by how much space the bindu claims, exactly as the cards' own text specifies ("small bindu," "medium bindu," "the largest bindu").

**`tattva-14` — Buddhi.** Small, precise bindu at the triangle's tip. Cool grey-blue line, crisp and minimal — discernment as clean edges.

**`tattva-15` — Ahaṃkāra.** Same composition, medium bindu now visibly claiming more of the triangle's interior. Slightly warmer tone (a muted rose-brown) — the "I" beginning to take up room.

**`tattva-16` — Manas.** Same composition, bindu largest of the three, nearly crowding the triangle's outline; add light, busy hatching immediately around the bindu (not elsewhere on the plate) to suggest proliferating, oscillating thought without cluttering the whole frame. Warmest of the three, closest to a dusty rose.

## The Ten Sense Powers (17–26, realm: material)

Two families of five: perception (jñānendriya, circular, ray-count rising 17→21) and action (karmendriya, square, ray-count rising 22→26). Each family runs through the same five-color element sequence in the same order — deep blue, green-gray, red, silver, gold — so the two families read as parallel structures, per the card's own note that hearing↔speech, touch↔grasping, sight↔locomotion, taste↔procreation, smell↔elimination are element-paired.

**`tattva-17` — Śrotra (Hearing).** Circle, five-petal lotus, bindu, and the *fewest* rays of the five perception cards — sound arrives without effort, so the plate should feel the quietest and most open. Deep blue.

**`tattva-18` — Tvak (Touch).** Same base, slightly more rays than hearing. Green-gray.

**`tattva-19` — Cakṣu (Sight).** Same base, more rays again, each ray ending in a small arrow-like point — vision as a sense that actively targets rather than simply receives. Red.

**`tattva-20` — Rasanā (Taste).** Same base, rays curving gently inward at their tips rather than pointing outward — taste as the sense that draws the world in. Silver.

**`tattva-21` — Ghrāṇa (Smell).** Same base, the *most* rays of the five — smell as the most anciently wired, most widely distributed sense. Gold.

**`tattva-22` — Vāk (Speech).** Square base (not circle — action senses are squares throughout, per the cards' own Yantra text), rays reaching outward from the square, fewest of the five action cards. Deep blue.

**`tattva-23` — Pāṇi (Grasping).** Same square base, more rays, reaching further outward — hands extending. Green-gray.

**`tattva-24` — Pāda (Locomotion).** Same base, more rays again, with a subtle directional lean (rays slightly denser on one side) evoking forward motion. Red.

**`tattva-25` — Pāyu (Elimination).** Same base, rays extending outward and slightly fading toward their tips — release, letting go, dispersing rather than reaching. Gold.

**`tattva-26` — Upastha (Procreation).** Same base, the *most* rays of the five action cards — maximum creative extension. Silver.

## The Five Subtle Elements / Tanmātras (27–31, realm: material)

Five plates, one shared form — a circle with a tiny bindu and a rising ring-count (3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7, exactly as each card's own text specifies) — growing denser and more saturated as the sequence approaches the gross elements. This set is the clearest place in the whole deck to visualize the accumulation doctrine directly: each plate should look like the previous one with one more ring added, nothing removed.

**`tattva-27` — Śabda (Subtle Sound).** Three concentric rings, tiny bindu. Faintest plate of the five — barely-there deep-blue line on the parchment ground, close to invisible at the edges (this is potential, not yet perceptible).

**`tattva-28` — Sparśa (Subtle Touch).** Four rings, tiny bindu. Slightly denser than sound, pale green-gray.

**`tattva-29` — Rūpa (Subtle Form).** Five rings, tiny bindu. Warmer and a shade more saturated — the first tanmātra "approaching visibility," so give it the faintest wash of red-gold, like light behind closed eyelids.

**`tattva-30` — Rasa (Subtle Flavor).** Six rings, tiny bindu. Denser still, silver tone with a soft inner glow — flavor as the quality of a moment, not a taste in the mouth.

**`tattva-31` — Gandha (Subtle Scent).** Seven rings, tiny bindu — the densest and most saturated of the five tanmātra plates, gold tone, innermost ring nearly solid. This is the tanmātra closest to matter; it should look the most "arrived" of the five, right at the threshold of the gross elements.

## The Five Gross Elements / Mahābhūtas (32–36, realm: material)

**`tattva-32` — Ākāśa (Space).** Two wide circles with vast open space between them, tiny bindu barely visible at center. The emptiest, most minimal plate outside `concept-anupaya` — deep blue line, very thin, most of the card left as bare parchment. This is the element that "contains everything by containing nothing."

**`tattva-33` — Vāyu (Air).** Two circles with eight interior rays, bindu at center. Green-gray line, rays reading as wind-motion (slightly curved rather than dead-straight) — more active than space but still light and mobile, never heavy.

**`tattva-34` — Tejas (Fire).** Two nested upward triangles, white-hot bindu at the shared apex. Saturated red, the single most intensely colored plate among the gross elements (the card's own text calls this "doubled intensity" — fire as transformation redoubled). The apex bindu should be the brightest point in the entire 49-plate set.

*(`tattva-35` Jala/Water already carries the 1858 Ganges-at-Haridwar PD lithograph — see Part 1.)*

**`tattva-36` — Pṛthvī (Earth).** Three nested squares, bindu at center, gold line — but per `concept-tanmatra-accumulation`'s own logic (earth alone holds every quality of the four subtler elements plus its own), this plate should carry faint traces of all four other element colors folded into the dominant gold: a whisper of deep blue, green-gray, red, and silver worked into the linework or the innermost square's fill, barely visible individually but giving the gold a richer, more complex warmth than any single-color plate elsewhere in the deck. This is the deck's visual argument for the accumulation doctrine made concrete on one card.

## The Five Realms (composite/level-2 cards)

All five composite realm cards share one stated base form in the grammar text — "a square containing a circle, a six-petal lotus, and a bindu" — so keep that nested container consistent across all five and differentiate purely by color/interior treatment, echoing the realm's own contents.

**`realm-pure`.** The base container rendered in the most luminous, least-colored way of the five — near-white or pale gold line, minimal shading. This is the realm before any contraction, so it should read as the "quietest" and most open of the five realm plates.

**`realm-coverings`.** Same container, but the lotus petals carry the five kañcuka colors (gold, green-gray, silver, deep blue, red) interwoven faintly around its edge — a direct visual echo of `tattva-12` Puruṣa's veiled-bindu treatment, since this composite card literally aggregates tattvas 6–12.

**`realm-psyche` already carries the Kashmir Śaivist manuscript PD image — see Part 1; no brief needed.**

**`realm-senses`.** Same container, but ring the lotus with ten small marks alternating in and out (five pointing outward, five pointing inward) — a compact visual reference to the five perception senses (receiving, inward) paired with the five action senses (acting, outward) that this card aggregates.

**`realm-elements`.** Same container, but render the circle as five concentric bands in the five element colors (deep blue outermost, then green-gray, red, silver, gold innermost) — this card is the direct composite portrait of `concept-tanmatra-accumulation`'s five-band diagram (see below) at realm scale.

## Concepts (level 1, category: concept)

**`concept-pratyabhijna` — Recognition.** Two nested circles, nearly the same size, with a bindu where they meet and eight rays breaking outward at that meeting point. Gold line; the outer circle can be drawn very slightly larger/offset from the inner one at first glance, with the rays visually "stitching" the two together — the moment of seeing they were never separate.

**`concept-svatantrya` — Absolute Freedom.** A circle containing a pure white (uncolored) bindu, with 24 fine rays radiating outward — the highest ray-count of any plate in the deck, deliberately exceeding even Ghrāṇa's or Upastha's ray-counts, since this card names the meta-capacity behind all the others. The bindu should be the one point in the whole 49-plate set left entirely without ink-tint — "the unconditioned core that no covering can reach."

**`concept-three-malas` — The Three Malas.** Three small bindus positioned at the corners of a faint, barely-drawn triangle — no other ornament. Each bindu takes one muted, unglamorous tone: āṇava (grey), māyīya (dull violet), kārma (rust-brown). Per the card's own text this "is not a yantra of power — it is the shape of forgetting," so keep this plate visually the sparest and least resolved of the concept cards; it should look unfinished on purpose.

**`concept-three-upayas` — The Three Upāyas.** Three concentric circles around a white bindu, each ring given a distinct line texture rather than a distinct color: the outer ring (āṇavopāya) in heavier, effortful cross-hatched line; the middle ring (śāktopāya) in a single light curved brushstroke, like a wave; the inner ring (śāmbhavopāya) barely visible, the thinnest line in the set next to `concept-svatantrya`'s bindu. Texture carries the meaning here, not color.

**`concept-anupaya` — No-Means.** A single thin circle outline on otherwise bare parchment — no bindu, no rays, no second shape. The emptiest plate in the entire deck, deliberately: per the card's own text, "every other card in this deck offers a form to contemplate. This one deliberately offers almost nothing."

**`concept-shadadhvan` — The Six Paths.** A vertical two-rail ladder motif: six horizontal rungs between two verticals, a bindu capping the top and another capping the bottom. Left three rungs (the objective/descending triad) rendered slightly heavier; right three rungs (the subjective/ascending triad) rendered slightly lighter, same line family and gold-on-parchment palette as the rest of the deck so it reads as one of the 49 despite being the most architectural, least yantra-like composition in the set.

**`concept-samkhya-comparison` — Why Thirty-Six, Not Twenty-Five.** The single most minimal plate in the deck apart from `concept-anupaya`: one plain vertical line, divided by a single horizontal band roughly two-thirds of the way down. Below the band: calm, unified, single-tone line (Sāṃkhya's 25 tattvas, unchanged). Above the band: the same line continues but with slightly denser, more worked linework (the 11 tattvas the Śaiva teachers add). No lotus, no bindu, no triangle — the card's own text explicitly withholds them, and the plate should honor that starkness.

**`concept-tanmatra-accumulation` — The Qualities That Accumulate.** Five concentric bands from a nearly invisible outer ring (sound alone) to a solid, richly textured, full-color innermost core (smell/earth, holding all five qualities at once) — exactly as the card's own Yantra text already specifies. This is the deck's master diagram for the accumulation doctrine; both `tattva-36` (Earth) and `realm-elements` above are meant to visually rhyme with this card rather than duplicate it outright — sparer echoes of the same idea, not copies of the same plate.

Cary-Yale Visconti Tarot — The Lavish Outlier (c. 1442)

Draw & reflectPlay it on recursive.ecoOpen in the card viewer

At a glance

An even earlier, extravagantly expanded Milanese game deck — possibly the oldest tarot fragment of all — notable for six ranks of court cards and for trumps depicting the three theological virtues (Faith, Hope, Charity) found almost nowhere else. A court game object, again from the Visconti world, again with no divinatory use.

Origin & dating

Held at Yale's Beinecke Library (the "Cary" of Cary-Yale; also called Visconti di Modrone). Giordano Berti dates it c.1442–1447 on numismatic grounds: the coin (denari) cards reproduce the gold florin struck by Filippo Maria Visconti in 1442 and withdrawn at his death in 1447. If correct, this is among the very earliest surviving tarot material.

Provenance & evidence

A fragmentary set (surviving cards number in the dozens). Its structural oddities are the key evidence: six courts (King, Queen, Knight, Jack plus a "Damsel" and a mounted "Lady"), and theological-virtue trumps that otherwise appear only in the much later Minchiate. This suggests the early trump set was not yet standardised — different courts experimented with how many triumphs to include.

Game or divination?

Game. The expansions are about enriching play and flattering a dynasty, not encoding secret doctrine. The virtue cards are conventional Christian moral allegory, the common visual currency of Renaissance courts.

The fear question

No fear — same logic as the Visconti-Sforza. Elite, heraldic, openly commissioned. If anything the Cary-Yale is more ostentatious (more gold, more courts), the opposite of something hidden.

Counter-voices

Because the Cary-Yale shares the theological virtues with Minchiate, some esoteric readers treat it as evidence of a lost "complete" sacred system later censored down to 22 trumps. The simpler, better-supported reading is the reverse: the early trump set was unfixed, and standardisation (not censorship) trimmed it over time.

Open questions / corrections owed

  • Whether the virtues replaced standard trumps or were additional is unsettled.
  • The c.1442 date leans on the florin argument; not all scholars accept it as decisive.

RESEARCH NOTE (2026-06-13). 67 cards survive of ~86 original. The six-rank courts (adding a Damsel and a Mounted Lady to King/Queen/Knight/Page) plus theological-virtue trumps make this the great outlier. NB a modelling issue: the extra court ranks currently collide on a four-archetype scheme (Damsel & Knave → page; Mounted Lady & Knight → knight) — needs distinct archetype ids. Bembo vs Zavattari attribution remains debated. See research/decks/cary-yale-visconti-tarot.md.

EDITORIAL CALL (2026-06-13): resolved the six-rank-court archetype collision — the Damsel now keys to `card:damsel-of-<suit>` and the Mounted Lady/Horsewoman to `card:horsewoman-of-<suit>` (was colliding with Page/Knight). 7 court cards re-keyed.

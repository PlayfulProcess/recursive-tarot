Minchiate — The 97-Card Florentine Tarot

Draw & reflectPlay it on recursive.ecoOpen in the card viewer

At a glance

A Florentine super-sized tarot game: 97 cards with 40 trumps instead of 22 — adding the zodiac, the four elements, and the remaining theological/cardinal virtues. Despite all that cosmic imagery it is a game, hugely popular for three centuries, and a flagship of the A-order branch.

Origin & dating

First recorded as germini in 1506 (named for the Gemini card, highest of the new trumps). The expanded 97-card pack so outsold the older form that the older name minchiate migrated to the big deck in the 1600s. From Florence it spread across Italy and into France through the 17th century.

Provenance & evidence

Many printed editions survive (e.g. the well-known 18th–19th-century Florentine Minchiate Fiorentine). Rules are preserved in gaming literature; pagat.com documents the game's structure. The expansion is the headline evidence: trumps nearly doubled to absorb astrological and elemental figures.

Game or divination?

Game. This is the deck that most tempts an esoteric misreading — zodiac! elements! virtues! — yet every record treats those cards as trump ranks in a trick-taking game, not oracle cards. The cosmology is decorative/encyclopedic, the same Renaissance visual vocabulary as the Mantegna prints, not a divinatory key.

The fear question

No fear. A mainstream parlor and gambling game for centuries. The familiar caveat applies — moralists disliked gambling — but Minchiate as such was respectable entertainment, openly printed and sold.

Counter-voices

Because Minchiate looks like a "complete" astrological tarot, occult writers sometimes present it as a survival of the true, larger sacred system that the 22-trump decks supposedly censored. The evidence runs the other way: the 22-trump form is older and the 40-trump Minchiate is an expansion for richer play, not a recovered esoteric original.

Open questions / corrections owed

  • Minchiate's zodiac/element trumps have no standard equivalent in 22-trump tarot — the grammar should resist forcing a one-to-one mapping (the repo already flags this).
  • Relationship between minchiate (77-card) and germini (97-card) naming needs a clean timeline in the grammar.

RESEARCH NOTE (2026-06-13) — the Papi. Scholarship identifies Minchiate's five lowest trumps as I=Bagatto (Juggler), II=Grand Duke, III=Western Emperor, IV=Eastern Emperor, V=Love — there is NO Pope card and the Popess is dropped (not folded in). Where this grammar names them Popess/Empress/Emperor/Pope/Love, prefer the scholarly identities (confidence medium; verify). The suit→element pairings are a modern overlay, not historical.

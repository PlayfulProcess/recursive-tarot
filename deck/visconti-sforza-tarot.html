At a glance

The oldest near-complete tarot we have: a luxury, hand-painted deck made for the ruling house of Milan around 1451. It is a game deck — the cards were still called trionfi, not "tarot," and certainly not used for divination. Its trump sequence is the headwater of the C-order branch that flows down to the Tarot de Marseille and, much later, the occult decks.

Origin & dating

"Visconti-Sforza" is a collective name for roughly fifteen related, incomplete sets from the mid-15th century. The best-known (the Pierpont-Morgan-Bergamo deck, c.1451) was commissioned around the marriage-alliance world of Filippo Maria Visconti, Duke of Milan, and his successor and son-in-law Francesco Sforza. Much of the painting is attributed to Bonifacio Bembo (or his workshop), an early-Renaissance Milanese miniaturist. Materials are unambiguously elite: tempera, tooled gold leaf, heraldic devices of the two dynasties.

Provenance & evidence

The cards are dispersed across the Morgan Library (New York), the Accademia Carrara and Colleoni collection (Bergamo), and elsewhere — hence the alternate name Colleoni-Baglioni. Several decks are missing trumps (one later set has only the Emperor and the Wheel surviving). The heraldry and the documented Visconti/Sforza patronage anchor the dating better than for almost any other early deck.

Game or divination?

Game, unambiguously. No divinatory apparatus, no fortune-telling text, no cartomantic tradition attaches to these objects in their own century. They were played as a trick-taking game by the Milanese court. Any "meaning" beyond the iconography is a later overlay imported in the 18th–19th centuries.

The fear question

No fear. This is conspicuous consumption, not clandestine ritual — a duke's heraldry is literally painted on the cards. Playing was a marker of status. The only contemporary anxiety around cards was the friars' campaign against gambling (see Bernardino of Siena), and even that targeted the wager, not the imagery. A 1451 Milanese courtier would have shown these off, not hidden them.

Counter-voices

The "secret Egyptian/Hermetic deck" reading has been retro-fitted onto Visconti imagery by later occultists, but nothing in the patron context, materials, or use supports it. The figures are standard Italian Christian-allegorical types (Pope, Popess, the Virtues, Death, the Wheel of Fortune) — courtly and Catholic, not crypto-pagan.

Open questions / corrections owed

  • Exact internal dating and workshop attribution of each of the ~15 sets is still debated; "c.1451" is a convenience label for the Morgan-Bergamo deck.
  • The original trump numbering was not printed on the cards; reconstructed order depends on later C-order witnesses.

RESEARCH NOTE (2026-06-13). Surviving cards split across Morgan Library (≈35), Accademia Carrara, Bergamo (≈26), and Casa Colleoni (≈13). The six later replacement cards are Fortitude, Temperance, Star, Moon, Sun, World — their attribution to Antonio Cicognara is discredited (it rests on a 19th-c. forgery); treat any 'second hand = Cicognara' note as superseded. Bembo vs the Zavattari workshop remains a live attribution debate. See research/decks/visconti-sforza-tarot.md.

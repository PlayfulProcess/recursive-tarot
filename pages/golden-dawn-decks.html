Golden Dawn decks: lineage and where to see them

Every deck below works from the same specification, Book T. Here is what each one is, how it stands to Book T, and where it can be seen. This library reproduces none of them: the modern decks are in copyright, so they are named and linked, never shown.

"Reportedly" marks a claim that rests on a single secondary source. Where two accounts disagree, both are given. The deck in this library is Book T read through the Rider-Waite-Smith pictures.

The story behind this list, from hand-copied cards to the 1978 fire at Whare Ra and why it was lit, is a chapter of the Golden Dawn course: Where the cards went: from Book T to the decks we have.

The Order's own cards

  1. c. 1888–1891 · printed 1912 Book T

    The Order's confidential paper on the tarot, mostly by S. L. MacGregor Mathers: titles, attributions and a description of each card. Crowley printed it in The Equinox I(8) in 1912. It is the text this library puts on every Golden Dawn card.

    Public domain The 1912 printing Card by card, here
  2. 1890s · lost The pattern deck

    One account, given in the Wang deck's published description, says it "would appear" Moina Mathers painted the original cards and that new Adepti Minores were lent them to copy by hand. Nick Farrell (2009) reads the record differently: the Outer Order had line drawings of some trumps, the Inner Order had written descriptions, and the Order never made a deck of its own. No pattern deck is known to survive.

    Not known to survive The first account Farrell's account
  3. 1930s, reportedly · cards lost in the 1960s Israel Regardie's own hand-painted deck

    The best documented member's copy. The cards vanished in the 1960s, reportedly stolen. Regardie kept photographs, gave them to Robert Wang as the guide for the 1977–78 deck, and Wang reportedly sold them to a private library connected with the O.T.O. Farrell writes that Regardie's personal cards are shown in Regardie's The Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic (1984); his note cites pages 10–12, which were not checked for this page.

    Photographs only A montage of the photographs Regardie 1984, in copyright
  4. 1912–1978 Whare Ra, Havelock North, New Zealand

    A Stella Matutina temple, one of the last descended from the Golden Dawn and the only one in a building made for it. Farrell asked older members and found no coloured deck: the only versions he saw were crude line drawings on small yellow cards, and many members took up the Builders of the Adytum cards in the 1960s. Most regalia and records were burned when the temple closed in 1978; by the account Nick Farrell gathered from members, one of the last chiefs decided it alone, to keep members' names from coming out. Pat Zalewski, who reportedly met former members around 1980, made line drawings of the Whare Ra trumps available online. Photographs of hand-painted Whare Ra decks were not found for this page.

    Line drawings online Whare Ra trumps, Hermetic Library The temple Why the papers were burned
  5. c. 1890–1937 W. B. Yeats's Golden Dawn papers

    Held by the National Library of Ireland, with a notebook of Golden Dawn and tarot material. Kathleen Raine's illustrated study Yeats, the Tarot and the Golden Dawn (Dolmen Press, 1972; revised 1976) works from them. Whether a full painted deck is among them was not confirmed.

    Archive NLI catalogue

Printed decks that came out of it

  1. 1909 Rider-Waite-Smith

    A. E. Waite's scheme, drawn by Pamela Colman Smith, both members of the Order. It follows the Golden Dawn order of Strength (VIII) and Justice (XI). Its imagery is the one this library uses.

    Public domain In this library
  2. painted 1938–1943 · book 1944 Thoth (Aleister Crowley and Lady Frieda Harris)

    A Golden Dawn descendant, heavily changed: several trumps renamed, Crowley's own court scheme and attributions. Harris's original paintings are at the Warburg Institute, London; the deck was first printed in the late 1960s.

    In copyright About the deck
  3. 1977 or 1978 The Golden Dawn Tarot (Robert Wang, under Israel Regardie)

    Painted by Wang from Regardie's photographs and the Order's papers, with Regardie sending comments on slides of each painting. Farrell calls it the first complete Golden Dawn deck. Listings give 1977 for the first issue and 1978 for U.S. Games; Wang's companion book, An Introduction to the Golden Dawn Tarot, is 1978.

    In copyright Publisher Card by card
  4. drawn 1975–1977 · published 1980 The Hermetic Tarot (Godfrey Dowson)

    Black-and-white pen drawings, published by U.S. Games (some listings say 1979). It stresses the Golden Dawn astrological attributions and follows Book T's descriptions loosely in places.

    In copyright Card by card
  5. 1991 · reissued 2000 or 2001 The Ciceros' deck (Chic and Sandra Tabatha Cicero)

    First issued as The New Golden Dawn Ritual Tarot (Llewellyn, 1991), later as the Golden Dawn Magical Tarot (sources differ on the year). Begun with Regardie's blessing before his death in 1985; by the makers' own account the first deck to use the Order's flashing colours and the colour scales of the four worlds.

    In copyright Card by card
  6. book 1997 and 2008 · deck 2022 The Magical Tarot of the Golden Dawn (Pat and Chris Zalewski)

    The Whare Ra line made public: Richard Dudschus redrew the Whare Ra cards with Pat Zalewski in the 1980s. The Zalewskis' book first appeared in a 150-copy run in 1997 with other drawings, by Jonathon A. Pierce; the 2008 Aeon edition used Dudschus's, and David Sledzinski coloured them for the deck issued in 2022.

    In copyright Card by card

The quickest way to see them

Book T's own words, then the Whare Ra line drawings at the Hermetic Library, then the steve-p.org pages, which set the Wang, Dowson, Cicero, Wendrich/Farrell and Zalewski cards side by side. For a hand-painted member's deck, Regardie's photographs are the one documented route.

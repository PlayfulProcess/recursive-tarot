## The three options, where no free source exists

- **(a) Link only.** The card shows no picture (or a drawn placeholder) and links to the holder's
  own page. Costs the deck its look; no rights question left.
- **(b) Keep it on the open site only.** The picture stays on tarot.recursive.eco, which is free and
  non-commercial, labelled with the holder's terms ("Source gallica.bnf.fr / Bibliothèque nationale de
  France: non-commercial reuse"), and is kept out of the recursive.eco app's paid tiers, prints and
  anything sold. The deck grammars already carry the Gallica credit; the app sync would need a flag.
- **(c) Reproduce and say so.** Show it everywhere, with a plain line on each card naming the source and
  its terms. For a non-commercial scan this is a choice to take a risk in the app and in print, not a
  licence; for a scan of a modern facsimile it is lawful in the US and the EU (a flat copy of a
  public-domain work gains no new copyright there), but the colours are a printer's, so say "from a
  modern facsimile".

## Charles VI (BnF): 17 cards

What the site shows: the BnF's scans (via its online exhibition and Commons, about 300 x 600 px),
free for non-commercial reuse with the Gallica credit; commercial reuse needs a BnF licence. The BnF
holds the only pack; no free **photograph** of any card was found (Commons' files all credit
expositions.bnf.fr; the Gallica drawings on Commons, "carte à jouer, dessin - btv1b…", are BnF scans
too).

The free sources found are **19th-century copies**:

- **1844 lithographs**: *Jeux de cartes tarots et de cartes numérales du quatorzième au dix-huitième
  siècle* (Société des bibliophiles français, Paris, Crapelet, 1844), 100 plates, opening with all
  seventeen cards hand-coloured after the originals. Copy: Université de Liège, DONum,
  <https://donum.uliege.be/handle/2268.1/15344> (open-access PDF, 50.5 MB). Rights line: the record sits
  in DONum's "Public domain" collection; the site's default is "Items in DONum are protected by
  copyright, with all rights reserved, unless otherwise indicated." So: filed public domain.
- **Chatto 1848**: *Facts and Speculations on the Origin and History of Playing Cards*, p. 198:
  engravings of La Justice and La Lune. Project Gutenberg 45584, Internet Archive
  `factsandspeculat45584gut` ("NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"), plates on Commons as "History of Playing Cards
  (1848) NN.png" ("Public domain").

## Visconti-Sforza: 20 trumps from the Dal Negro facsimile (and the rest)

What the site shows: 23 Commons files "Scanned from the Dal Negro reproduction: I Tarocchi dei
Visconti" (20 trumps plus three group nodes), about 360 x 720 px; and 87 files "scanned by David
Madore", which the Commons credit says are scans of the AGMüller facsimile. **So every Visconti-Sforza
picture on the site is a copy of a modern printed facsimile.** Commons tags them "Public domain".

The cards: 35 at the Morgan (MS M.630; it offers a low-resolution download per card for "personal,
educational or noncommercial use", unaltered and fully captioned), 26 at the Accademia Carrara and 13
in the Colleoni collection, Bergamo (no online images found). Which trump is where was confirmed only
for five Morgan cards: Love, Time (the Hermit) and the three later replacement cards, Temperance
(M.630.5), Fortitude (M.630.8) and the Sun (M.630.13). The Morgan's pages could not be read (its site
blocks automated reading), so the rest of its list is for her to check at
<https://www.themorgan.org/manuscript/158525>.

Free photographs of the real cards on Commons are few and tiny: the Fool (File:Visconti-Sforza, The
Fool.jpg, 170 px, "Own work", "Public domain", shot at the Morgan) and the Popess (File:Bonifacio
Bembo.jpg, 300 px, "Public domain", credited to trionfi.com, captioned as the Morgan's). One 2025
phone scan of the World (Pierre André Leclercq, 1,868 px, "Public domain") was taken in France,
so presumably of a printed copy.

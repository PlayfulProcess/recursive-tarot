## (a) The readings of Book T

### The texts

| Text | What it is | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Book T**, as printed in *The Equinox* I(8), 1912, pp. 143-210 | The Order's own tarot paper, set down mainly by Mathers (c. 1888-1891), printed by Crowley. Describes the 4 Aces, 16 courts and 36 small cards picture by picture; for the 22 Keys gives only titles, attributions and meanings. | Public domain | [scan](https://archive.org/details/IAPSOP-equinox_v1_n8_1912_Sep) · [this site's reading page](https://tarot.recursive.eco/pages/book-t.html) |
| Mathers, *The Tarot* (1888) | Mathers's **public** booklet for fortune-telling: not Book T. Uses Lévi's letters (the Magician is Aleph) and keeps Justice 8, Strength 11. | Public domain | [sacred-texts transcription](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/mathers/index.htm) |
| Regardie, *The Golden Dawn* (1937-40; Llewellyn editions since) | The Order's papers in print, Book T among them, with further tarot material. The usual way practitioners now read Book T. | In copyright; not read for this note | Llewellyn |
| Waite, *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot* (1911) | A Golden Dawn member's own reading, describing the Rider-Waite-Smith pictures; prints Strength 8, Justice 11. | Public domain | on every Book T card here, in its own section |
| Case, *An Introduction to the Study of the Tarot* (1920) | The Builders of the Adytum line: Golden Dawn letters with Waite's numbers. | Public domain | on each Key here |
| Companion books of the later Golden Dawn decks: Wang (U.S. Games deck 1977-78; *The Qabalistic Tarot*, 1983), the Ciceros (Llewellyn, 1991 and later), the Zalewskis (book 1997/2008, deck 2022) | Each fills in what Book T leaves out, most of all the Keys' pictures, from other Order papers or its makers' own designs. | In copyright | [Steve P.'s comparisons](https://www.steve-p.org/cards/GDaw.html) · [Farrell, "Creating a Golden Dawn Tarot", 2009](http://www.jwmt.org/v2n17/farrell.html) |
| Crowley, *The Book of Thoth* (1944), with Harris's Thoth deck | The best-known reading derived from Book T. | In copyright | O.T.O. |

### Where they differ

1. **Strength and Justice.** Book T 1912 gives Fortitude the letter Teth and Leo, Justice Lamed and
   Libra, but keeps the old numbers (Fortitude 11, Justice 8). Waite's deck (1909) is the first
   known to print Strength 8 and Justice 11. Mathers 1888 has neither change. The Thoth keeps the
   1912 arrangement (Lust XI, Adjustment VIII). Whether an Order manuscript renumbered the two before
   1909 is not settled by any printed source checked here
   ([the repo's note](https://github.com/PlayfulProcess/recursive-tarot/blob/main/research/synthesis/strength-justice-numbering.md)).
2. **Letters.** Book T puts Aleph on the Fool; Mathers 1888, after Lévi, on the Magician. Crowley
   later swaps the Emperor and the Star (Tzaddi and Heh) on the authority of his *Book of the Law*;
   no Golden Dawn source does.
3. **The Keys' pictures.** Book T 1912 describes none. Golden Dawn decks draw them from elsewhere:
   Regardie's own hand-copied deck (lost, photographs went to Wang), Westcott's papers (court-card
   line drawings, published 1996), Whare Ra's line drawings, or their makers' designs. One account
   has Moina Mathers paint the Order's pattern deck; Farrell argues the Order never had a deck of its
   own. Both stand.
4. **The courts.** Book T: Knight (on a horse, the highest), Queen, Prince (in a chariot), Princess.
   Waite renames them King, Queen, Knight, Page, which has long confused how the two systems map.
   The Thoth keeps Book T's four.
5. **Small cards.** Book T pictures each as a hand from a cloud holding the suit's symbols, with
   roses, lotuses or flames, the decan's planet and sign above and below. Waite and Smith draw
   scenes; the Thoth draws abstract arrangements under shortened titles ("Dominion", "Ruin").
6. **Text variants.** The 1912 Ace of Pentacles reads "four Crosses" where other printings read
   "Roses" (noted in `tarot/book-t/PROMPTS.md`). Regardie's text was not compared line by line here.

### How her rebuilt Book T compares

Her deck, [Book T: the Golden Dawn's 78 cards](https://tarot.recursive.eco/viewers/cards.html?src=../tarot/book-t/grammar.json),
takes **only** the 1912 printing as its authority:

- **Order and names**: Book T's own list of 78 titles; Book T's titles as card names.
- **Numbers**: as 1912 prints them (Justice 8, Fortitude 11), against the Waite habit.
- **Small cards, Aces, courts**: pictures painted from Book T's sentence for that card, then
  traced; counted cards (Swords 4-10, Cups 7-10) composed by script so every sword and cup is as
  Book T counts them; a third picture set places the decan's glyphs where Book T's sentence places
  them.
- **Keys**: with no description to follow, the prompts start from each Key's Book T title. These are
  her least Book-T-bound cards, and the place where the Order's other papers, and the published decks,
  most differ from her.
- **Ace of Pentacles**: follows the 1912 scan ("Crosses"), showing only the two buds.
- **Beside Book T**: Waite (1911) and Case (1920) in their own named sections, never mixed in.

So her reading is a strict 1912 reading. The live questions for practitioners are the Keys, the
Crosses/Roses variant, and whether any Order manuscript supports the 1912 numbering or the Waite
one.

## (b) The d'Este pictures: what quality exists, and where

- **Yale is the only holder found**: Beinecke Library, Cary Collection, PLAYING CARDS GEN 966,
  [record 33215685](https://collections.library.yale.edu/catalog/33215685). 16 cards ("Imperfect: 16
  cards only"), 140 x 78 mm, hand coloured, with embossing; fronts and backs, 32 images.
- **Full size is 3,256 x 5,640 px per card** (IIIF level 2, TIFF/JPG/PNG). This site shows
  1,100 x 1,905 px. **The better-quality pictures she wants already exist, at the same holder,
  about three times sharper**: `https://collections.library.yale.edu/iiif/2/<image id>/full/full/0/default.jpg`
  (image ids 33215686 to 33215716, even numbers for fronts).
- **Rights**: Yale's open-access policy ("may be used by anyone for any purpose", for works believed
  public domain) covers them; Yale's record adds a general liability warning. Credit "Yale University
  Library".
- **No facsimile edition** of the d'Este cards was found (the Yale-related facsimiles are of the
  Cary-Yale Visconti deck, by U.S. Games and Il Meneghello). No Commons category for them was found.
- **Related but different**: the Rothschild cards at the Louvre (about 31 cards, Florence c. 1435-40
  per the Louvre's 2026 notice) sometimes come up beside the Ferrarese cards; photographs
  © GrandPalaisRmn.
- **Dating**: Yale catalogues "[1450?]". The Este arms on the court cards have been read as those of
  Ercole I d'Este and Eleonora of Aragon, which would put the pack around 1473
  ([Into Mystery](https://intomystery.substack.com/p/identifying-the-figures-on-the-deste);
  [Pollett](https://l-pollett.tripod.com/cards84.htm)). The site says c. 1450 with the 1470s noted.

So the d'Este question for the community is not "where are better pictures" (Yale has them) but
"is anything else known": other photographs taken before conservation, sibling cards elsewhere,
the dating.

## (c) Where these are discussed, and what each expects

| Where | URL | What it expects from a newcomer |
|---|---|---|
| Tarot History Forum | https://forum.tarothistory.com | The historians' forum (Dummett's and Decker's readers). Search old threads first (Book T, Strength/Justice, the Este cards have threads); cite sources with page numbers; history, not divination. |
| International Playing-Card Society, *The Playing-Card* journal | https://i-p-c-s.org | Collectors and scholars of the objects. Precise object data: holder, shelfmark, size, technique. Membership for the journal; enquiries by email. |
| The World of Playing Cards | https://wopc.co.uk | Collectors' reference site with a contact form; good for identifying printings (the early Waite-Smith editions). |
| Associazione Culturale Le Tarot (Andrea Vitali) | https://www.letarot.it | Italian tarot history, Ferrara and the Este court included; essays in Italian and English. Write with sources, ideally in Italian. |
| Tarot Heritage (Sherryl E. Smith) | https://tarot-heritage.com | A historian's blog on early decks; questions in the comments of a relevant post. |
| Journal of the Western Mystery Tradition | http://www.jwmt.org | Golden Dawn practitioners' journal (Farrell's 2009 essay on Golden Dawn decks is there). Writes from inside the tradition. |
| *Correspondences* (journal for the study of Western esotericism) and the ESSWE | https://correspondencesjournal.com · https://www.esswe.org | Academic: framed as research, with literature. |
| TarotForum.net | https://www.tarotforum.net | Large reader community with deck-specific threads, Golden Dawn and Thoth boards. Friendly to practice questions. |
| Reddit r/tarot | https://www.reddit.com/r/tarot/ | General readers; fine for "how do you read the Keys". Not a history venue. |
| Yale Library, Ask a librarian | https://ask.library.yale.edu | For the d'Este cards themselves: conservation photographs, provenance files. Short, specific, with the call number (PLAYING CARDS GEN 966). |

Common to all: say who you are and what you made, link the page, ask one concrete question, show
what you already checked, and don't post AI-made pictures as if historical (her Book T pictures are
labelled as new; keep that label in any post).

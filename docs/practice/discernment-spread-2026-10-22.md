# Test the Spirits — a discernment spread

In the Caster as "Test the Spirits" (`viewers/spreads.json`).

There is no way of telling which of the invisible voices that speak to us is telling the truth. Most people take it for granted that if a message comes from the invisible world, it is something special and should be obeyed. Three old traditions answer with the same move: don't ask where a voice comes from. Ask where it leads, and what it grows.

This spread lays a message out along that road. It never says whether to obey. The deck's rule holds here as everywhere: relate to the card; never obey it.

## The three sources

**Matthew 7:16.** "Ye shall know them by their fruits." The fruits are named in Galatians 5:22–23: "love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance."

**Ignatius of Loyola, rules for the discernment of spirits** (*Spiritual Exercises*, 1548; Elder Mullan's translation, 1914). His rules for the Second Week are written for exactly this case, a voice that sounds holy:

- The bad angel can come "under the appearance of an angel of light", bringing good thoughts first and turning them later (Rule 4).
- So watch "the course of the thoughts": if "the beginning, middle and end is all good", trust it; if it ends somewhere worse, or takes away your peace, it is not to be followed (Rule 5).
- The good spirit touches a soul going toward the good "like a drop of water which enters into a sponge"; the other, "as when the drop of water falls on the stone" (Rule 7).
- Resolutions made in the glow after a consolation "have need to be very well examined" before they are acted on (Rule 8).

And from the First Week: "In time of desolation never to make a change" (Rule 5).

**The Kalama Sutta** (Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.65; Bhikkhu Sujato's translation, CC0). The Kalamas ask the Buddha how to tell which of the visiting teachers speaks the truth. He tells them not to go by lineage, scripture, logic, "the appearance of competence", or the thought that the teacher is respected. "But when you know for yourselves" that a thing leads "to harm and suffering", give it up; and take up what leads to welfare and happiness.

Three traditions, one test: the source doesn't settle it; the course and the fruit do.

## The spread

Seven cards in three rows (PlayfulProcess's arrangement, 22 Oct 2026, evened out). The top row is what arrives: the voice (1) and the name it claims (2). The middle row is the road it asks you to walk: beginning, middle, end (3, 4, 5). The bottom row is what it does: how it touches you (6) and what it bears (7).

```
        [1]       [2]
   [3] → [4] → [5]
        [6]       [7]
```

| | Position | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The voice** | What is asking to be followed? A message, a hunch, a teaching, a dream. |
| 2 | **Whose name it speaks in** | The authority it leans on: a lineage, a scripture, a teacher, the invisible. The Kalama test: this card can't settle anything. Notice how much weight you want to give it. |
| 3 | **Beginning** | How the voice first came: what it offered, what it promised. |
| 4 | **Middle** | Where following it takes you once the first glow fades. |
| 5 | **End** | Where the road comes out. Ignatius: is it as good as where it started, or less good than what you had meant to do? |
| 6 | **Sponge or stone** | How it touches you: soft, quiet, like water into a sponge; or sharp and noisy, like water on stone. |
| 7 | **Fruits** | What it would grow in you and around you. Love, joy, peace, patience, gentleness? Or harm, to you or to others? |

**If the reading itself leaves you in desolation, change nothing today.** Keep the resolution you held yesterday, and come back to the question later.

**The decision stays yours.** The spread can show the shape of a road; it can't walk it, and it can't tell you who is speaking.

## Choices made (22 Oct 2026, left to Claude by PlayfulProcess)

- **Seven cards.** The Celtic Cross has ten and fits the board; card 2 (the authority) and card 6 (sponge or stone) carry the Kalama and Ignatius tests, so they stay.
- **The name stays "Test the Spirits"** (1 John 4:1, "try the spirits whether they are of God"). It names the source honestly; the Kalama Sutta in the description keeps it open to readers of other paths.
- **A spread only**, in the Caster's list. No course page and no new voice for now: the lightest form first.

# Crowley & Harris, the Thoth Tarot — full chapter

## At a glance

The Thoth Tarot is the second great child of the Golden Dawn's **Book T**, after the
Rider-Waite-Smith. **Aleister Crowley** (1875–1947) designed it, **Lady Frieda Harris**
(1877–1962) painted it between 1938 and 1943, and Crowley explained it in **The Book of Thoth**
(1944). It keeps Book T's skeleton (the Hebrew letters, the paths of the Tree, the decans of the
small cards, the four courts) and changes what Crowley thought a new age required: card names,
one pair of letters, and the Judgement card.

This chapter answers three questions: why the name *Thoth*, what Crowley said he was doing, and
what changed from Book T. The deck's pictures are referenced, never reproduced here; the
paintings and the deck are held in copyright by the O.T.O.

## Who made it

- **Crowley and the Golden Dawn.** Crowley was initiated into the Golden Dawn on 18 November 1898
  and, by his own account, reached the grade of Practicus three months later, the grade that
  entitled him to see the secret attributions of the trumps [@crowley1944_thoth, "The Evidence
  for the Initiated Tradition", §4 "The Nature of the Evidence"; later citations to "The Nature
  of the Evidence" mean this subsection]. In 1900 he was Mathers's agent in the Battle of Blythe Road (chapter 12).
- **Crowley published Book T.** The one public printing of Book T used in this library, *The
  Equinox* I(8), September 1912, was Crowley's own magazine, and it carries his notes, marked
  "O.M." or "Fra. P." [@equinox1912_bookt]. So the text the Thoth deck departs from is a text
  Crowley himself had broken the Order's secrecy to print. The whole of it is on
  `pages/book-t.html`, with his notes set as notes.
- **Frieda Harris.** Clifford Bax introduced Harris to Crowley on 9 June 1937; she was 60. She was
  studying Rudolf Steiner's Anthroposophy and, from 1937, projective geometry with Olive Whicher
  and George Adams, which is where the deck's lines and planes come from. She joined the A∴A∴ and
  the O.T.O., paid Crowley a stipend through the project, and found backers for the exhibition and
  the printing [@web_frieda_harris_wp].
- **Six months became five years.** Crowley meant a short update of the traditional pictures; the
  work ran from 1938 to 1943 [@web_thoth_tarot_wp]. By Crowley's own account, reported in the
  same article, it was Harris who pressed him to put his whole system into the cards
  [@web_frieda_harris_wp].
- **Book and deck came apart.** *The Book of Thoth* appeared in 1944 in a limited edition from the
  Chiswick Press, as *The Equinox* volume III, number 5 [@crowley1944_thoth]. The paintings were
  photographed and printed as a deck only in 1969, after both had died [@farley2009, p. 142].

## Why "Thoth"

The name comes from the French origin myth, not from Egypt.

- **Court de Gébelin (1781)** proposed that tarot was the surviving book of the Egyptian god Thoth
  (chapter 09). **Etteilla** built his whole practice on it and sold his deck as the *Livre de
  Thot* (chapter 10a). **Éliphas Lévi** joined tarot to the Hebrew letters. Farley traces this
  Egyptian story from French Egyptomania through the later occultists, Crowley among them
  [@farley2011].
- **Crowley's subtitle keeps the myth:** *A Short Essay on the Tarot of the Egyptians.* His
  section on the trumps is headed "The Atu of Tahuti", glossed as the houses or keys of Thoth,
  the Egyptian god of wisdom, magic, science "also Illusion" [@crowley1944_thoth, "The Atu of
  Tahuti"].
- **And his history abandons it.** The book's opening chapter says the origin of the pack "is very
  obscure", lists the Egyptian and the gypsy theories without taking either, and declines "to
  enter into any discussion of these disputed points" [@crowley1944_thoth, "The Origin of the
  Tarot"].

So the title is a lineage name. It places the deck in the line Gébelin, Etteilla, Lévi, Golden
Dawn, and claims that line's prestige, while the book itself does not claim an Egyptian source.

## What Crowley said he was doing

Crowley moves the question of authority from history to system, and then from system to
revelation. In his own summary:

> The origin of the Tarot is quite irrelevant, even if it were certain. [@crowley1944_thoth,
> "Summary of the Questions Hitherto Discussed"]

He says the cards must stand or fall on their own merits, as "a deliberate attempt to represent,
in pictorial form, the doctrines of the Qabalah", and compares the evidence to a crossword: once
the right letters are in, the fit proves itself (same section). Three intentions follow from the
book:

1. **To show the Tree of Life in the cards.** The "only theory of ultimate interest", he writes,
   is that the tarot is a symbolic picture of the universe built on the Qabalah ("The Theory of
   the Correspondences of the Tarot"). This is Book T's programme, carried on.
2. **To bring the tarot into the New Aeon.** Crowley dated a new age to 1904, when he received
   *The Book of the Law* in Cairo. He wrote that it would be "absurd to attempt to carry on the
   outworn traditions", and set himself to keep what was eternal in the tarot while updating the
   "dogmatic and artistic features" that had become unintelligible (the passage on the Last
   Judgement, in "The Twenty-two Keys, Atu, or Trumps of the Tarot"). The renamed cards and the new Aeon card are this
   intention.
3. **To prove *The Book of the Law*.** Crowley presents the one change he made to Book T's letters
   (below) as "the most convincing evidence possible" that his 1904 book is "a genuine message
   from the Secret Chiefs" ("The Nature of the Evidence").

What the claims rest on, by the book's own account: the Golden Dawn's attributions (which he
says came from the Cipher Manuscripts); Lévi, who in Crowley's telling knew the right
attributions and hid them under an oath; gematria; his own revelation of 1904; Frazer's *Golden
Bough* for the courts; and, through Harris, Steiner's geometry. No Egyptian document is among
them.

### Errors of fact in the book

Crowley's history is loose where it can be checked:

- He writes that packs survive from the fourteenth century. The earliest surviving tarot cards
  are fifteenth-century Milanese (chapters 01 and 02).
- He dates the Golden Dawn's founding to 1886. The order was founded in 1888 (chapter 12).
- He accepts that the Cipher Manuscripts carried a genuine tradition and that Anna Sprengel
  authorised the order. Decker and Dummett argue the Sprengel letters were fabricated
  (chapter 12).

## What changed from Book T

| | Book T (*The Equinox* I(8), 1912) | Thoth (Crowley & Harris; *The Book of Thoth*, 1944) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fortitude / Lust** | Numbered 11; letter Teth, Leo | **XI Lust**; Teth, Leo. Renamed: "Lust implies not only strength, but the joy of strength exercised" ("XI. Lust") |
| **Justice / Adjustment** | Numbered 8; letter Lamed, Libra | **VIII Adjustment**; Lamed, Libra. Renamed |
| **Emperor and Star** | Emperor: He, Aries. Star: Tzaddi, Aquarius | **Letters swapped**: Emperor takes Tzaddi, Star takes He; the signs stay (Aries, Aquarius) |
| **Judgement** | Key 20 | **XX The Aeon**, "an adaptation of the Stele of Revealing", the Egyptian funerary stele at the centre of Crowley's 1904 revelation ("XX. The Aeon") |
| **Other trump names** | Magician, High Priestess, Wheel of Fortune, Temperance, Universe | Magus, Priestess, Fortune, **Art**, Universe |
| **The Fool** | 0, Aleph, at the head of the trumps | Kept: Crowley calls putting 0 before 1 "the secret of the initiated interpretation" ("The Nature of the Evidence") |
| **Courts** | Knight, Queen, Prince, Princess | Kept, as Father, Mother, Son and Daughter of the Tetragrammaton; the Father is a Knight "because he is represented as riding on a horse" ("The Tarot and the Elements") |
| **Suit of Earth** | Pentacles | **Disks** |
| **Small cards** | Two to Ten on the decans, each a "Lord of …" title | Decans kept; the titles cut to one word printed on the card (Book T's "Lord of Ruin" is the Ten of Swords' "Ruin"). Some are new words, such as the Six of Swords' "Science" for Book T's "Lord of Earned Success". *These titles are from the deck as published; not re-checked against the book here* |

Two of these need care.

- **The Strength–Justice numbers did not move.** The Thoth deck keeps Book T's printed numbers
  (Justice 8, Fortitude 11) with Book T's letters. It was Waite's deck of 1909 that renumbered the
  two (`research/synthesis/strength-justice-numbering.md`). Crowley still describes the pair as a
  "counterchange" needed to keep the zodiac in order.
- **The Tzaddi swap is Crowley's own.** While writing down *The Book of the Law* on 8 April 1904,
  he says, he asked whether he had the attributions right and was answered: "All these old letters
  of my Book are aright; but Tzaddi is not the Star" (the book prints the Hebrew letter). Years
  later he decided Tzaddi belonged to the Emperor. The result, in his words, is a second loop in
  the zodiac sequence: Aries and Aquarius turn about Pisces as Leo and Libra turn about Virgo
  ("The Nature of the Evidence"). Book T has no such swap.

## Game or divination?

**Esoteric/initiatory.** The book treats divination as a lesser use and the deck as a map of the
universe and of the Great Work, as Book T did. The deck's later life is mostly divination.

## Counter-voices

- **Waite and Crowley moved authority the same way.** Waite wrote that "there is in fact no
  history prior to the fourteenth century", and called the Egyptian, Indian and Chinese origin
  stories deception and self-deception; in the same preface he held back the cards' deeper
  meaning for "those who have received some part of the Secret Tradition", citing "a question of
  honor" [@waite1910, preface]. Crowley gives up the history too, then rests the deck on a
  revelation made to himself. Neither claim can be checked from outside: both men take authority
  from history and place it in initiation. That move is the historian's main reservation about
  both decks; it is not a reason to doubt the internal coherence of either system.
- **Orientalism.** The New Aeon is announced by an Egyptian stele, and the book glosses Thoth with
  the remark that "the Hindu and Scandinavian Gods corresponding are debased forms" ("The Atu of
  Tahuti"). That is the imperial habit chapter 12 names: other cultures' gods borrowed for
  prestige and ranked below the borrower's synthesis.
- **Harris's share.** Book T supplied the system and Crowley the doctrine, but the look of the
  deck, its geometry and colour, is Harris's; by Crowley's account she also widened the project.
  Histories that call it "Crowley's deck" undercount her.

## Legacy

- With the Rider-Waite-Smith, the Thoth deck is one of the two main modern descendants of Book T,
  and the source of a family of later decks and handbooks (Lon Milo DuQuette's guide among them).
- The renamed trumps (Lust, Art, Adjustment, Aeon) and the one-word small-card titles became the
  vocabulary of a second reading tradition beside Waite's.

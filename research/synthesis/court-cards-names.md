# The court cards: which Book T card is which Rider-Waite-Smith card

Checked Nov 26 2026, after a reader of the printed Book T deck asked why its court cards do not
match the Rider-Waite-Smith ones. There are two honest answers. Which one is right depends on
what you match: the name and the picture, or the place in the family.

## Book T says it itself

Book T heads the court cards "The Four Kings", "The Four Queens", "The Four Princes" and "The
Four Princesses". The Kings are "Figures mounted on steeds"; the Princes are "Figures seated in
Chariots"; the Princesses "are the Knaves of the Tarot Pack" (*The Equinox* I(8), 1912; public
domain; `research/sources/book-t-equinox-1912.txt`).

Then, under the first court card, the 1912 printing adds a note:

> Note that the Kings are now called Knights, and the Princes are now called Kings. This is
> unfortunate, and leads to confusion; the Princes may be called Emperors without harm.
> Remember only that the horsed figures refer to the Yod of Tetragrammaton, the charioted
> figures to the Vau.

So the same printing uses two lists of names:

| The figure | Book T's headings | The 1912 card list | Element |
|---|---|---|---|
| A rider on a horse, the Father | King | Knight | Fire of the suit |
| A queen on a throne, the Mother | Queen | Queen | Water of the suit |
| A young man in a chariot, the Son | Prince | King | Air of the suit |
| A standing young woman, the Daughter | Princess | Knave | Earth of the suit |

## Crowley, *The Book of Thoth* (1944)

Crowley says the Qabalists named them King, Queen, Prince and Princess, but "they were also
called Knight, Queen, King and Princess" (p. 18–19). For the Thoth deck he chose Knight, Queen,
Prince and Princess, and gives the reason: "The Father is 'Knight' because he is represented as
riding on a horse" (p. 19). In Part Three (p. 150–151) the Prince is the son of the Queen "by the
Knight who has won her", and the old King "becomes a young Knight" when the story starts again.

## The two ways to match them to the Rider-Waite-Smith deck

Waite printed the traditional names (King, Queen, Knight, Page) and does not state which element
each one is.

**By name and picture.** The Waite-Smith Knights ride horses, the Kings and Queens sit on
thrones, the Pages stand. Match the pictures and the 1912 names together:

| Book T | Rider-Waite-Smith |
|---|---|
| Knight (rider, Fire) | Knight |
| Queen | Queen |
| Prince (1912: "King") | King |
| Princess (1912: "Knave") | Page |

**By place in the family.** Book T's own headings call the rider the King, the Father. Match the
ranks (the oldest man with the oldest man):

| Book T | Rider-Waite-Smith |
|---|---|
| Knight (the Father) | King |
| Queen | Queen |
| Prince (the Son) | Knight |
| Princess | Page |

Most Thoth-to-Waite tables use the second match; the Wikipedia article on the Thoth deck does, and
its talk page argues about it. Forum discussions (thelema.org posts 14110 and 66831; the Tarot Forum
blog "The Court Cards Conundrum") give both.

## What this deck does

The recursive.eco Book T deck uses the 1912 card list on the cards themselves (Knight, Queen, King,
Knave), and the printed booklet gives each court card Book T's own rank and element under its name
(Prince of Wands · Air of Fire). Neither table above is wrong. A reader moving from a Waite deck can
keep one rule in mind: **the horseman is the Fire of the suit and the Father; the chariot rider is
the Air of the suit and the Son.**

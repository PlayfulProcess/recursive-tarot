# Book T paintings checked against Book T's words (Nov 28 2026)

PlayfulProcess asked for one more pass before the Game Crafter print, because the Ace of Wands is wrong: Book T gives its branches three, four and three flames, and the painting has one flame per branch.

**What was checked.** All 78 paintings as printed: the JPEG embedded in each framed card (`tarot.recursive.eco/tarot/book-t/painted-symbols/<id>.svg`), against the first section of each node in `tarot/book-t/grammar.json` on `main` (Book T, *The Equinox* I(8), 1912). For the 56 Aces, courts and small cards, every countable or visible detail was compared: flames, yods, wands, cups, swords, roses, buds, pentacles, hands, what each hand holds, crests, animals, colours, ground. The planet and sign Book T sets above and below the small cards are left out of every painting on purpose (they are in the frame), so they are not counted as missing. **Book T describes no picture for the 22 Keys**, only a title, an attribution and a meaning, so the Keys were checked only against those: does the picture carry its title and its sign.

**Result.** 2 cards are wrong in a way a reader will notice; 11 have small departures; the other 65 match.

- **Wrong count:** Ace of Wands (one flame per branch instead of 3 / 4 / 3).
- **Painted marks:** Queen of Wands (a row of pseudo-letters along the bottom edge is still in the printed painting; the window in the framed card shows the whole height).
- **Minor** (11): Ace of Swords, Knight of Wands, Princess of Wands, Queen of Cups, Prince of Cups, Princess of Cups, Prince of Swords, Princess of Swords, Queen of Pentacles, Eight of Pentacles, Three of Swords.

Severity: **wrong count** = a number Book T gives is not what is painted; **missing element** = a named object or figure is absent; **marks** = painted text-like marks; **minor** = present but placed, coloured or shaped differently from the words.

## Worth repainting, most important first

1. **Ace of Wands** (`wands-ace`), wrong count. Corrected prompt line:
   > A white radiant angelic hand rises from clouds at the bottom and grasps a heavy wooden club that divides into three branches. The left branch ends in exactly three separate small flames, the centre branch in exactly four separate small flames, the right branch in exactly three separate small flames: ten distinct flame tongues in all, each branch's flames side by side at its tip like the points of a crown. Around it twenty-two small leaping flame drops: three below the right branch, seven in an arc above the centre branch, six above and six below the left branch.

   The model may still merge flames; if it does, the deck's composite method (paint one flame tongue once, place ten by script, as was done for Swords 4–10) gives an exact count. The 22 yods already in the painting are right and could be kept.
2. **Queen of Wands** (`wands-queen`), painted marks. No repaint needed: crop the bottom strip (about the lowest 140 px of the 1248 px original) before the frame is built, or re-run with the line "the ground below her throne is plain green grass with nothing written or drawn on it".
3. **Queen of Pentacles** (`pentacles-queen`), optional. The dark half of the face is a brown block. Re-run line: "Seen in strict profile, the half of her face toward the viewer is lit pale, the far half and the edge of her jaw fall into deep shadow, a soft clean division down the face."
4. **Three of Swords** (`swords-03`), optional. Re-run line: "The central sword's point has split the five-petalled rose into two halves that fly apart; the petals scatter and fall; no stem, no rays."
5. **Queen of Cups** (`cups-queen`), optional. Re-run line: "An ibis stands close beside the throne, tall enough that her left hand rests on its back, and in that hand she holds a lotus."

The other eight minor departures (Ace of Swords, Knight of Wands, Princess of Wands, Prince of Cups, Princess of Cups, Prince of Swords, Princess of Swords, Eight of Pentacles) are not worth the credits before the print; they can wait for a later round. A note on the Ace of Wands' left and right: Book T's "right" can mean the card's own right (the viewer's left). The painting puts the twelve yods at the viewer's left and the three at the viewer's right; whichever reading she prefers, the corrected prompt above keeps the count and can be mirrored.

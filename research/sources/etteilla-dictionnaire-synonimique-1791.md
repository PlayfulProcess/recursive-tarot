# Etteilla's words on the 78 leaves: the *Dictionnaire synonimique du Livre de Thot* (1791)

**What this file is.** A transcription of the head-words of the "Table des synonimes du Livre de Thot,
suivant l'ordre des feuillets" (pp. 19-57), one upright word and one reversed word for each of the 78
leaves of Etteilla's *Livre de Thot*, with this site's English translation. It is the source the
three Etteilla decks' `Upright` and `Reversed` sections are written from
(`scripts/archive/etteilla_own_words_2026_10_08.py` reads the table below).

**The book.** *Dictionnaire synonimique du Livre de Thot, ou synonimes des significations primitives
tracées sur les feuillets du Livre de Thot; précédé d'un discours préliminaire par un membre de la
Société des Interprètes de cet ouvrage.* Paris: chez Etteilla fils / Samson; Lyon: Hugand, 1791.
Public domain. Copy used: Bibliothèque interuniversitaire de santé (Paris), cote 55509, scanned on
archive.org as [`BIUSante_55509`](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_55509) (Licence ouverte /
Etalab). A second scan of another copy: [`1791dictionnairesynonimique`](https://archive.org/details/1791dictionnairesynonimique).

**Whose words.** The book is anonymous, by "a member of the Society of Interpreters"; the
archive.org record of the second copy says correspondence of the school suggests the author is
probably Joubert de La Salette, a pupil of Etteilla (reportedly; not checked here). The *head-words*
are Etteilla's: the preface (p. 4, scan [`n7`](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_55509/page/n7/mode/1up)) calls them "les significations primitives qui ont été imprimées
& scellées sur chaque feuillet du Livre de Thot par notre savant Maître, M. Etteilla". The long
lists of synonyms under each head-word are the Society member's, not Etteilla's, and are not
copied into the decks; each card links to its page instead.

**How it was transcribed.** From the archive.org OCR, every head-word checked by eye against the
page images (the OCR is rough: it reads the long s as f). Spelling as printed, with the long s
written as s ("Eclaircissemens", "Connoissance", "Robbe", "Misere" are the book's spellings).
The page column is the book's printed page; the scan's page `nN` is printed page + 3
(page 19 is [`n22`](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_55509/page/n22/mode/1up)).

**Two irregularities in the printing.** Leaves 9 and 71 print their second head-word without the
"R." (renversé) mark that every other leaf carries; it stands where the reversed word stands, and
is taken as the reversed word here. Leaf 8 spells the name "Eteilla", and its heading line also
carries the word "Repos" (7th day of creation); leaf 1 reads "Etteilla".

**Numbering.** Leaves 1-21 are the trumps, then the Fool ("F. 0", Folie, card 78 in the decks
here), then the four suits from King down to Ace: Bâtons 22-35, Coupes 36-49, Épées 50-63,
Deniers 64-77 (King, Queen, Knight, Page, then Ten down to Ace). This is the order of the decks'
item ids (`etteilla-01` ... `etteilla-78`, `oracle-dames-01` ...).

**What the decks get from this.** Etteilla I (*Livre de Thot*, 1789) is the deck these words were
printed on. Etteilla II (Blocquel, 1838-1845) and III (c. 1865) are later editions whose own
printed captions may differ; they carry the same words, labelled as Etteilla's for the 1789 leaf,
and their own captions are not transcribed here.

**Not reached.** Etteilla's *Manière de se récréer avec le jeu de cartes nommées tarots*, third
cahier (1783-1785), where he first set out his meanings, is on Gallica
(ark:/12148/bpt6k312882c, also bpt6k622723) but Gallica answered this session's requests with a
security check (HTTP 403), so it was not read. The Wellcome Collection's scan
([b28777323](https://archive.org/details/b28777323)) holds cahiers I-II only. The English
translation of the third cahier at thirdcahier.blogspot.com (`web_dodoucet` in
`research/bibliography.bib`) is modern and is cited, not quoted. Nor were the captions printed on the
Etteilla II and III cards read: doing that from the card images would be a machine transcription,
which the repo does not use as a tradition's text.

## Translation notes (this site's)

- *Propos* (3): talk, remarks; the synonyms run from "dessein, intention" to "parole, discours", so
  both senses are given.
- *Force Majeure* (14): not the modern legal term; the synonyms are "puissance extraordinaire,
  force physique ... impulsion violente".
- *Misere* (19): the synonyms are "indigence, pauvreté, disette", so "destitution" rather than
  "misery".
- *Étranger* (25): the synonyms are adjectives first ("étrange, inusité, inaccoutumé, inconnu"),
  so the leaf is the strange as much as the stranger.
- *Ennui* (46): the eighteenth-century sense, "déplaisir, mécontentement ... chagrin léger", not
  boredom.
- *Nouvelle Connoissance* (46 R.): the synonyms are "nouvelle instruction, nouvelle information",
  so "new knowledge" rather than "new acquaintance".
- *Critique* (56): the synonyms open with "position fâcheuse, moment critique" and go on to
  "censure, blâme", so both senses are given.
- *Effet* (69): "suite, conséquence, résultat" and also "biens, meubles, immeubles".
- *Argent* (71): "richesse, somme, monnoie; argenterie, blancheur", and "Lune".
